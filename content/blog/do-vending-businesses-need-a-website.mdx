A lot of vending operators run profitable businesses with no website at all. Referrals, cold visits, and a good reputation carry them. So the honest question is not "can you survive without one." It is "how many placements are you losing without knowing it."

## Who is looking you up

Three groups search for vending operators:

- **Property and office managers** who were told to "get a vending machine for the breakroom." They search, compare two or three operators, and call the one that looks established.
- **Business owners selling or buying machines**, if you deal in equipment.
- **Your own prospects**, after you leave a card. They search your company name before calling back.

If the search turns up nothing, or a page that looks ten years old, you lose the quiet comparison you never knew was happening.

## What a vending website needs to do

A vending site does not need to be fancy. It needs to do four jobs:

### 1. Prove you are real and local

Your service area, the kinds of locations you serve, a few photos of clean machines, and a phone number that works. Structured data so Google shows you for "vending services near me."

### 2. Make the placement request easy

A short form: name, business, address, roughly how many employees or visitors. That form should land in your inbox and, ideally, your [CRM](/services/crm) so it becomes a pipeline deal immediately.

### 3. Sell machines, if you sell machines

If you sell or lease equipment, each machine type deserves its own page with specs, photos, and a quote form. Those pages rank for "buy vending machine [your city]" and bring in buyers who never would have called.

### 4. Load fast on a phone

Most of these searches happen on a phone between tasks. A slow site gets abandoned. A fast one gets a call.

## What it should not do

It should not require you to update it. It should not cost you a designer every time you add a service. It should not be a "link in bio" that forwards to Facebook.
