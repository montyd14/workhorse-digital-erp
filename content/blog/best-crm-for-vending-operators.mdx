If you have tried a generic CRM for your vending business, you already know the problem. It wants a "company" and a "contact." You have locations, machines, commission rates, and a property manager who changes every eighteen months. The CRM was built for someone selling software subscriptions, not someone placing a snack machine in a warehouse breakroom.

Here is what a CRM built for a **vending operator** should do, and how to decide between buying one off the shelf and having one built around your routes.

## What a vending CRM actually needs

### Locations, not just contacts

The unit of a vending deal is the location. Every record should start with an address, foot traffic, hours, and the type of business. Contacts attach to the location. When the office manager leaves, the location stays and the new contact goes on it.

### Pipeline stages that match placement sales

A typical placement pipeline looks something like this:

1. Prospect identified
2. Contacted
3. Site visit done
4. Proposal sent
5. Contract signed
6. Machine placed

If your CRM cannot show you how many locations are at each stage, it is a contact list, not a sales tool.

### Commission and contract terms on the record

Many placements involve a commission to the location. The CRM should store the rate, the payout schedule, and the contract end date so renewals never sneak up on you.

### Follow-up reminders you will actually see

Property managers are busy. Deals close on the fourth or fifth touch. The CRM needs to tell you who to call today, on your phone, while you are on the route.

### A link to the rest of the business

The best setups connect the CRM to your [website](/services/websites) so placement requests land in the pipeline, and to your [operations dashboard](/services/erp-dashboards) so a signed contract turns into a machine on a route without re-typing anything.

## Off-the-shelf vs. built for you

| | Off-the-shelf CRM | Built for your operation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup time | Days, then months of workarounds | Four to six weeks |
| Fits vending language | Rarely | Yes, it is built on your terms |
| Per-user pricing | Usually | Flat fee |
| Connects to your routes | Only with extra tools | Designed in from the start |
| Risk | Monthly fee whether it works or not | Built first, pay only if you keep it |

Off-the-shelf makes sense if you have a handful of locations and a lot of patience for configuration. If you are growing past a few dozen machines and spending hours a week in spreadsheets, a system built around your routes pays for itself quickly.
